# Editorial notes — fallback source material

Used when `--source` is omitted. Replace this file or point `--source` at
verified notes for the topic you are publishing.

Working definition: "cheap" means a free or low-cost entry tier, not a promise
that a product is free forever. Pricing, limits, and features change; do not put
exact prices in a published video unless a human verifies them first.

## Five practical tools

1. ChatGPT — a writing and planning partner for first drafts, customer reply
   templates, offer ideas, checklists, and meeting summaries. Review every
   factual or customer-facing answer before sending it.
2. Canva — turn one offer into a flyer, square post, story, or simple deck. Keep
   brand colours and a human review step.
3. CapCut — short clips, automatic captions, resizing, simple social edits.
   Confirm export limits, watermarks, and licensing for the current plan.
4. Google Gemini — brainstorming, rewriting, and organising work that already
   lives in the Google ecosystem. Do not paste private customer data into a tool
   without checking the account's data controls.
5. Zapier — move a form response into a spreadsheet, notify a team member, or
   create a follow-up task. Start with one trigger and one action; check task
   limits and every connected app's permissions.

## Audience

Hawaii-based small businesses, nonprofit staff, and local learners. Examples can
include a food truck, repair shop, independent retailer, community organisation,
or service provider. Be respectful and specific without tourist stereotypes. The
video is educational: no revenue, savings, or investment promises.

## Teaching goal

Leave viewers with one rule: pick one repetitive task, try one tool, review the
output, and measure whether it saved time. Check current pricing, privacy terms,
and licensing before adopting anything.
